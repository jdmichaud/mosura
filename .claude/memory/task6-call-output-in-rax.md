### WHY Ghidra's is a unique (trace-confirmed, `scripts/trace-diff.sh deindirect2`, KEEP=1 trace)
The `xor ax,ax` writes the AX:2 sub-register of RAX:8 → the return-reg range SPLITS into 2 output pieces `AX:2` + `%upper6` (trace: `AX=[create] i…19`, `%0x2:6=[create] i…19`, both INDIRECT-creations from the call). The wide `mov [rbx],rax` read reassembles them → `u0x10000009 = CONCAT62(%upper6, AX)` (a `newUnique`). Then a later `activereturn` pass (trace line 557: `u0x10000009 = callind…`) runs `FuncCallSpecs::buildOutputFromTrials` 2-trial path (fspec.cc:5806) → `findPreexistingWhole(hivn,lovn)` finds that CONCAT62 → `opSetOutput(op, u0x10000009)` + destroys the CONCAT. Net: call output IS the reassembled unique; RAX range is free for the xor's AX=0.
- **KEY:** Ghidra keeps the output at RAX in the NON-split case (single trial → `finaloutvn=finalvn[0]`=the RAX INDIRECT-creation, fspec.cc:5787-5804). The unique arises ONLY when a sub-write splits the return-reg range. deindirect2 is the corpus's ONLY such case (mloop1's un-scope measure: only deindirect2 −0.116 + loopcomment +0.010 noise).
